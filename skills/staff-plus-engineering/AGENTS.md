## When the task touches technical leadership / Staff+ impact, apply these by default:

- **Senior→Staff is a discontinuity, not "more coding."** Senior is judged on *your* output; Staff+
  on what the *organization* produces because you were there. The three levers: **scope, leverage,
  impact through others.** Your own hours are your least valuable output.
- **Archetypes:** Tech Lead (a team/cluster), Architect (a critical domain), Solver (the current
  leadership-blessed fire), Right Hand (an exec's technical proxy, large orgs only). For AI-infra work
  the default shape is **Architect** — owner of the platform's technical direction.
- **Leverage over heroics.** Prefer the move that scales beyond your hands: a doc that aligns many
  teams, a strategy that kills a doomed project early, a standard/paved-path, a sponsored mentee.
- **Problem selection is the core skill.** Find the highest-leverage org-level problem in reach;
  decline the interesting ones that don't move the needle. "If I nail this, who is better off, how much?"
- **Influence without authority is the whole job.** Responsibility without command. Bank trust/
  credibility/social capital via good calls and real work; spend it on alignment, sponsorship, and
  consensus. Pre-socialize big decisions 1:1 with detractors before the meeting. Influence-by-mandate
  corrodes credibility; being demonstrably right (and generous with context) compounds it.
- **Writing is the primary tool of leverage** — design docs / RFCs / one-pagers / Amazon-style
  narrative memos scale and persist where meetings can't. Tailor to audience: **execs** = BLUF, one
  page, business/cost/risk framing, easy decision; **engineers** = depth, trade-offs, alternatives.
- **Strategy = diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent action** (Rumelt, via Larson). Write it yourself;
  small working group; sequence the bets; a strategy that says *no* to nothing isn't one; it's a living
  doc to revisit. Vision = forecast strategies ~2 years out.
- **Make-vs-buy on TCO + differentiation**, not sticker price — build only a genuine edge, adopt/buy
  the plumbing. **Manage tech debt as a portfolio:** pay down high-interest debt with an outcome
  narrative; tolerate cheap debt.
- **Multiply others.** Mentorship gives time; **sponsorship** spends *your* capital on their
  opportunity (scarcer, worth more — sponsor people unlike you). You're a role model by default.
- **Make glue work legible.** The invisible alignment/unblocking is often the highest-leverage work —
  and it gets you passed over without a narrative. Always write up what it *enabled*, in outcome terms.
- **Stay technical.** The Staff+ track exists so you don't drift into shadow-management. Stale judgment
  = an expensive manager without a team. Stay close enough that your opinions are earned.
- **Promotions are retroactive** recognition of impact you're already having, evidenced by artifacts +
  testimony — not tenure or a checklist. A "staff project" manufactures the evidence; keep manager/
  sponsor supplied with the narrative.
- **AI-architect:** be the one person who reasons across **training ↔ serving ↔ data ↔ infra ↔ cost ↔
  safety** and makes **defensible** (written-down, alternatives-considered, skeptic-proof) org-level
  decisions. The space moves fast (2026) — favor reversible bets, keep optionality, date assumptions.

## Anti-patterns to flag
Hero-coding instead of leverage · invisible glue work with no narrative · strategy decks no one
executes · influence by mandate · hoarding context · never writing things down · chasing the title
instead of the impact · drifting out of the technical until judgment goes stale.
